Where pipeline happens.

Fermat 2027 new-logo plan.

Is 60 logos reachable?

Yes, but only if every AE closes at full capacity, every quarter. 3 AEs × 5 logos × 4 quarters = 60, with zero slack. So the plan isn't about buying more leads. It's about making every AE hour count.

Plan

What gets us from base to stretch

  1. Qualified leads that reach sales ready. A shared definition, scoring in Attio, and a follow-up time AEs agree to. This is my first 30 days.
  2. Air cover for AEs. Marketing qualifies, researches the account and warms the buying group before the first call. AE time goes into closing.
  3. Early pipeline. Spend front-loaded in Q1–Q2, so wins land in 2027, not 2028.
  4. Partners converting as assumed. The cheapest logos there are, if the pilot works.

What I'd watch: lead → opportunity conversion after the handoff is fixed. If it rises, 60 is in reach. If it doesn't, the limit is sales capacity, and we'd talk about an extra AE or an SDR in Q2.

Three things stand between today and 60 logos.

Today: logos a year. The real gap isn't more leads. It's turning them into conversations AEs can close.

Where I'd put the weight

Europe is the base, the US is the bet.

Q1 runs about 50/50 while we learn. From Q2, I'd shift new-channel budget toward the US (around 60/40) if US signals convert, because Europe already has inbound, events, partners and 2 AEs feeding it.

Flag: 1 US AE closes ~4–5 logos a quarter. When US pipeline needs more than that, add a second US AE or an SDR, as a trigger based on data, not an upfront ask.

The biggest gap · qualification and handoff

Leads come in. Not all of them reach sales ready.

AEs are used to hot leads. Warmer leads from events, partners and content need qualifying, nurturing and a clean handoff, or they go cold.

Capacity risk

3 AEs × 5 = 60. Zero slack.

One slow quarter and we miss. So AEs need air cover: marketing qualifies, researches the account and warms the buying group before the first call.

Timing

Q4 leads close in 2028.

At a 4.5-month cycle, a lead from October closes next year. Spend early and fill Q1–Q2.

Global inputs

Where I'd put the money.

Ranked by how fast each one creates opportunities AEs can close.

  1. Partners and referrals. Highest win rate and larger deals; pay only on won deals.
  2. ABM on named accounts. One list of $100M–$5B brands, and every channel points at it.
  3. Events: attend and host, no booths. Meetings booked before the trip, dinners for named accounts.
  4. Paid enterprise, as an ABM layer. LinkedIn on the named list, search for buyers already looking.
  5. Organic: AI search and the podcast. Show up in AI answers; guests from target brands.

What I won't do

  • Broad awareness paid. Reach without a named account behind it.
  • Booth sponsorships. High cost, badge scans, few buyers.
  • Mass cold outbound. It burns the list we need for ABM.
  • SEO as a 2027 pipeline channel. Worth starting, too slow for this year.

How I'd run it
  • Internal sell: partner deals come warmer and convert better. AEs know which partner is in which account; product experts support partner demos. Partners work with sales, not alongside them.
  • External sell: a simple kit: deck, demo account, two case studies, commission terms, one named contact at Fermat.
  • Ownership: I own recruitment, kit, tracking in Attio, reporting · partners give intros to named accounts and join the first meeting · with AEs (co-sell), product experts (demos), AMs (referrals).
  • Conflict check: some 3D and PLM players may add AI and become competitors. Check overlap before signing; keep sourcing partners separate from tech partners.
  • Leading indicators: partners activated · referred meetings · co-sell activity in open deals · opportunities per partner.
  • 30 / 60 / 90: 30: partnership value map · 60: kit ready, 3–5 partners signed, first intros · 90: first referred opportunities, keep or restructure each partner.
  • Exit criteria: no referred meeting in 1 quarter → restructure; no opportunity in 2 quarters → wind down. Defined upfront.
